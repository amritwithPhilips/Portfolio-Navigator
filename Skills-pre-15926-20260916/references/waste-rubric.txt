Scoring rubric — what each 1–5 score means, per waste condition							
Each condition is scored 1–5 on its own scale. 1 = the waste condition does not apply (healthy); 5 = it fully applies (worst). The italic line under each condition is the plain-language question; pick the option below that best matches the project.							
							
#	Waste condition	Weight	"1 — Does not apply
(healthy)"	2 — Largely clear	3 — Partial / mitigated	4 — Substantially applies	"5 — Fully applies
(worst)"
1	"New investment in sunset technology
Are we spending new money to build on — or add to — a system we've already agreed to retire or replace?"	20%	No — the money goes to a system we plan to keep and invest in. It's fully supported and part of our target set-up.	Mostly not — it's a system we tolerate for now, with more than three years of vendor support left and no decision to retire it.	Partly — the system is marked for replacement, but this spend only keeps it running normally while a funded replacement is already under way.	Largely yes — new build or customisation on a system marked to be replaced or retired that is still supported today, or loses support within a year.	Yes — brand-new build or customisation on a system we've agreed to retire (or that is already out of support): new money poured into what we're leaving.
2	"Investment off the target architecture
Does this grow a system that sits outside our agreed future ('target') set-up, instead of moving us toward it?"	15%	No — it fits our agreed future set-up and moves us toward it.	Mostly fits — it's on the agreed set-up, with only small, approved exceptions.	Off the agreed set-up, but a dated plan in this year's budget will bring it back in line.	Grows a system outside the agreed set-up; a move back is mentioned on the roadmap but isn't funded or dated in this plan.	Grows a system outside our agreed future set-up, with no plan at all to bring it back in line.
3	"Duplicate of an enterprise capability
Are we paying for something a shared, company-wide system already does?"	10%	No — it does something unique that no shared, company-wide system offers.	Only a small overlap — it fills a real gap the shared system can't cover, under an approved exception.	Partly overlaps a shared system, but there's a plan to consolidate or retire one of them.	Largely duplicates a shared, company-wide system, justified mainly by local preference or timing.	Yes — it's a local copy of something a shared, company-wide system already does in full.
4	"No or weak business case
Is the spend hard to justify — no clear benefits, no owner, or it won't pay back in time?"	15%	Strong case — benefits are measured and checked, someone owns delivering them, and it pays back well within the system's life.	Good case — benefits measured with a named owner and payback within the system's life, though the numbers are only lightly tested.	Half a case — either the benefits are measured or an owner is named, but not both; payback is plausible but unproven.	Weak case — benefits are mostly words not numbers, no clear owner, and payback is uncertain or close to the system's end of life.	No real case — no measured benefits, nobody accountable, or it won't pay back before the system is retired.
5	"Low strategic fit
Can we actually connect this to a stated company or business priority?"	15%	Strong fit — it directly delivers a named company or business priority.	Good fit — it supports a priority indirectly, and that link is written down.	Loose fit — linked to a priority in words, but not traceable to a specific goal.	Weak fit — the link is vague or implied; it's driven more by keeping things running than by strategy.	No fit — no traceable link to any company or business priority; it's local preference or habit.
6	"Incremental fragmentation
Is this yet another small one-off add-on, rather than part of a planned, bundled improvement?"	10%	No — it's part of a planned, bundled release, not a one-off.	Standalone, but genuinely a one-time need with no pattern of similar requests.	One of a few similar requests, with a proposal to bundle them into a planned release.	One of a recurring stream of similar requests handled one at a time; bundling has been discussed but not agreed.	Yes — another small one-off in a steady stream of look-alikes: the 'treadmill' that never gets bundled into a proper plan.
7	"Paying for unused capacity
Are we buying more licences or capacity while a lot of what we already own sits unused?"	5%	No — what we already own is more than 90% used, and the extra is fully backed by real demand.	Mostly not — usage is 75–90% and the extra matches confirmed demand.	Some slack — usage is 60–75% and the extra partly goes beyond current demand, or we haven't checked whether we can reuse what we have.	Clear slack — usage is only 50–60% and we're still buying more, or we could reclaim unused licences but aren't.	Yes — we're buying more while less than half of what we already own is actually used.
8	"Deepens lock-in / raises exit cost
Does this make us more dependent on a supplier we plan to leave — harder or costlier to exit later?"	5%	No — it reduces lock-in (portable, standards-based), or it applies to a system we intend to keep.	Neutral — it adds no new supplier dependency.	Adds some supplier dependency, but on a system we plan to keep, so exit cost isn't the worry.	Raises the running or exit cost of a system we plan to leave, but only modestly and recoverably.	Yes — it clearly raises the running or exit cost of a system we plan to leave; every euro spent makes leaving harder.
9	"Fully deferrable
Could we simply postpone this for a year or more with no real harm?"	5%	No — there's a hard deadline this year (legal, security, or contract); it can't wait.	Barely — a dated commitment within about six months, and slipping it causes real harm.	Somewhat — useful this year but could slip 6–12 months with manageable impact.	Largely — no firm date; it could slip up to a year with only minor impact.	Yes — it could wait a year or more with no dated business, legal, or security impact.
Weighted waste score = Σ (weight × score) across all nine conditions, on the same 1–5 scale.  A validated legal/regulatory, security, or quality mandate is never exited on score alone — a high score triggers a minimum-viable-compliance scope challenge instead.
