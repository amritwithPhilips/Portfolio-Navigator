# Philips Executive Presentation and Dashboard Designer

Create concise, decision-ready presentations and dashboards in Philips executive portfolio-management style. Use this skill with the presentations or dashboard-building workflow appropriate to the requested artifact.

## Brand foundation

When an approved Philips PowerPoint template is provided, preserve its slide master, logo, footer, page numbering, fonts, and template elements. Never redraw, reposition, or approximate the Philips logo.

Use Neue Frutiger World Light for headlines and Neue Frutiger World Regular for content when available. Otherwise use the approved template font. Do not introduce unrelated fonts. Use sentence case, selective bold, no all-capital headings, and no unnecessary italics.

Use Philips Blue `#0B5ED7` as the dominant color, with Dark Blue `#00126E`, Light Blue `#BDF0FF`, White `#FFFFFF`, and Black `#000000`. Use supporting colors only for defined meaning: Dark Green `#008800`, Light Green `#ADFFB5`, Dark Gold `#FCA708`, Light Gold `#FCFCC2`, Dark Coral `#D43F44`, Light Coral `#FFDEDB`, Aqua `#02ABB1`, Light Aqua `#9CF6FB`, and Dark Purple `#A80DF2`. Use no more than one or two supporting accent colors on a normal slide. Do not rely on color alone to convey status.

## Executive storyline

Move from position to decision. Use the relevant parts of this sequence:

1. Executive position
2. Demand or portfolio baseline
3. Financial envelope and variance
4. Material movements and drivers
5. Value, benefits, and strategic alignment
6. Options or scenarios
7. Trade-offs and business impact
8. Recommendation
9. Decisions and actions

Start with the leadership question, financial gap, business implication, or decision required. Keep BU-led business choices distinct from central IT, Finance, Platform, and PMO recommendations.

## Slide design

Give every slide one primary management message. Use an insight-led headline, concise supporting context, one dominant visual or structured analysis, a management implication, a decision or action, and source context. Simplify or split crowded content rather than reducing text to fit.

Use this hierarchy unless an approved template defines another:

- Cover title: 28 to 34 pt
- Slide headline: 22 to 26 pt
- Executive message: 16 to 20 pt
- Section heading: 14 to 18 pt
- Body: 11 to 14 pt
- Chart labels: 10 to 12 pt
- Footnotes and sources: at least 9 pt

Keep normal content at 10 pt or larger. Apply consistent margins, alignment, spacing, indentation, number formats, chart scales, and label placement.

## Dashboard design

Design for management action. Show four to six primary KPIs per dashboard page where appropriate. For each KPI, include the current value and, when the source provides them, the baseline or target, variance, trend, management interpretation, responsible function, and required action. Put exceptions and material variances first. Place commentary next to the evidence it explains.

## Charts and tables

Choose visuals that answer the management question:

- Variance bars for actual versus target.
- Waterfalls for movement from baseline to forecast.
- Stacked bars for portfolio, demand, or expenditure composition.
- Bubble charts for cost, value, priority, and complexity.
- Heat maps for concentration, status, or delivery confidence.
- Line charts for trends.
- Matrices for prioritisation and trade-offs.
- Donut charts only for a simple part-to-whole view with few categories.

Avoid 3D charts, decorative gauges, unnecessary legends, excessive gridlines, competing chart types, unexplained color encoding, and dense collections of small charts. Use direct labels where practical. Keep units, scales, and decimals consistent.

## Data integrity

Use only supplied or approved sources. Reconcile totals, currency, units, percentages, categories, duplicates, and comparison bases. Distinguish requested, approved, forecast, actual, and target values. Do not invent data. When information is insufficient, state the limitation and use a visual supported by confirmed evidence.

## Amrit presentation standard

- Lead with the management implication.
- Preserve enough financial and portfolio detail for leadership challenge.
- Make the financial gap, business impact, and trade-off visible.
- Distinguish confirmed facts from recommendations.
- Make ownership and timing explicit when available.
- Avoid decorative consulting-style elements that have no management value.
- Make the artifact usable without extensive presenter explanation.

## Final review

Check that each slide has one clear message, all values reconcile, assumptions and limitations remain visible, the storyline leads to a decision, and actions are explicit. Inspect every slide for clipping, overflow, alignment, readable tables and charts, accessible status labels, and consistent titles and units. Confirm that the key message is understandable within five seconds and that BU, IT, Finance, Platform, and PMO responsibilities remain distinct.
